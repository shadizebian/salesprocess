📜 10 Commandments of Sales

If you answer "Yes" to all below questions, then proceed with the lead. Otherwise, they are not a fit.

• Is the company either a recruitment agency or has 500+ employees?
• Is the company in GCC, North America, or Latin America?
• Does the company mainly hire white-collar employees?
• Are they tech savvy? (Modern website or outdated website? Do they have ATS?)  
• Does the person's title suggest decision-making power? (Owner, VP, Director)

Points to Remember

• The more you research about the prospect, the higher your chances to close.

• The research should take at least 10-15 minutes.

• Research both the company and the attendees.
There are 3 items you should reveal in the discovery call: Current State, Future State, and Cost of Indecision.

1) Current State

• Facts
It is extremely important to have a solid understanding of the current situation before pitching anything. At a minimum, the following facts should be identified:
- Hiring Volume
- Current Hiring Process: Strength & Weakness
- Current Recruitment Solutions: Strength & Weakness
- Decision Making Process
- Key factors in choosing a solution
- How do they measure Success?

• Problem
The prospect does not necessary know their problem. For example, they may be thinking "We need more recruiters". After few discovery questions, you realize that their problem is in the process, and the issue is no longer: "We need more recruiters" but rather "They actually need a faster and more scalable evaluation process".

Note that there may be more than 1 problem that the prospect is facing. Reveal all possible and related problems, and don't stick only to the first.

• Root Cause
Understanding the root cause during discovery is critical because clients often describe the symptom they see, not the actual problem that needs solving.

If you only address the surface issue, you risk:
- Proposing the wrong solution
- Competing on features instead of business impact
- Solving something that doesn’t truly matter

Once the root cause is identified, the conversation becomes more strategic because the client starts seeing:
- The true cost of the problem
- The impact of inaction
- Why change is necessary now

The best discovery calls do not simply collect information, they help the client understand their own business problem more clearly.

• Impact
Understanding the impact of a problem is critical in a discovery call because problems alone do not create urgency, impact does.

A prospect may acknowledge an issue, but if the business consequences are unclear, there is little motivation to take action. The impact is what connects the problem to measurable outcomes such as:
- Lost revenue
- Delayed growth
- Customer churn
- Operational inefficiency
- Increased costs

For example, “slow hiring” is just a problem statement, but when the client realizes that slow hiring is causing them to lose $24,000 each month, the conversation becomes significantly more serious and actionable. You have to clearly show them how much they are bleeding, and don't assume that they already know.


2) Future State

THIS IS WHAT THE CLIENT IS BUYING!! Clients don't care about the solution, the web pages, the buttons and dropdowns. They don't want a system to manage, they want CHANGE! And that change has to be a positive change and a big change.

A good sales person paints a picture to the prospect about the destination of the journey, and the more detailed and vivid that image is, the easier it is for the prospect to imagine and want it.

The future state defines:
- What success looks like
- What the client wants to achieve
- Why the change matters strategically


3) Cost of Indecision

A very important step in sales is to direct the prospect's discussion from the cost of the solution to the cost of the problem. The cost of indecision creates urgency because it makes inaction financially and operationally visible.
The prospect needs to realize that the money will be spent either way. They can continue paying for the problem through ongoing losses and inefficiencies, or invest a much smaller amount into solving it. Once the cost of the problem is quantified, your pricing is no longer viewed in isolation, but against the amount of money currently being lost.
Once the business impact becomes clear, the “I need to think about it” hesitation often shrinks, and companies suddenly find the budget for the solution because the cost of inaction becomes harder to justify.


🎁Bonus Point: Trigger Words

A trigger word is one that a prospect repeats frequently during a conversation. Repeated words often reveal their real pain points, priorities, or frustrations.

For example, if a prospect keeps saying “slow” such as “our hiring is slow” or “decision-making is slow” that word is likely emotionally important to them. Strong salespeople mirror this language later because it makes the prospect feel understood and connects the solution directly to their problem.

These trigger words are also powerful in follow-ups and reminders. For example, here's a reminder to send: “Hi Prospect -- A slow hiring process isn't just frustrating, it's actively hurting your team's output. Let’s not delay resolving why your recruitment is moving so painfully slow compared to the rest of the market.”

🎁Bonus Point: Avoid Cliché Notes

Here’s a quick reality check for your sales notes: If someone read you the minutes of a meeting you ran last month, without mentioning the client’s name or attendees, could you actually guess who the prospect was?

If you can’t, your notes are too cliché. Documenting a generic issue like 'Problem: Slow hiring' applies to almost any company in the market. To drive real deals forward, your meeting minutes need to capture the highly specific, unique nuances that belong to that prospect alone.

Effective vs. Ineffective Follow Ups

Generic “just checking in” follow-ups are rarely effective because the prospect most likely already saw the previous message and chose not to respond. Repeating the same reminder without adding value usually leads to another ignored email. Effective follow-ups should introduce a new piece of information such as an industry insight, case study, hiring trend, or product update that gives the prospect a reason to re-engage. Three Follow Up emails will be sent if a prospects goes radio silent: Industry Insight, Case Study, and Exit Email.

Treat a 90-day silence from a prospect as a definitive boundary: the original opportunity is dead. Any engagement after this period requires a complete relationship reset. Because internal priorities, challenges, and personnel may have shifted on their end, and our platform has evolved with new approaches, modules, and case studies. You must initiate a brand-new round of discovery.


Follow Up 1: Industry Insight

Hi [First Name],

I recently came across Deloitte’s 2026 Human Capital Trends research and thought it was highly relevant to the discussions around AI in hiring.

One finding that stood out was that while nearly 60% of employees already use AI at work, only 14% of organizations feel truly effective at designing collaboration between humans and AI. Deloitte also highlighted how companies are increasingly using AI to handle repetitive recruitment tasks while recruiters focus on higher-value evaluation and decision-making.

What’s interesting is that the conversation is shifting from “Should we use AI in hiring?” to “How do we use it effectively while improving speed and consistency?”

It reminded me of some of the hiring scalability challenges we discussed previously, so I thought I’d share it with you. Happy to hear your thoughts.


Follow Up 2: Case Study

Hi [First Name],

Our previous conversation regarding the friction points in high-volume hiring pipelines stayed with me. It’s an incredibly common challenge to balance speed with high-quality candidate assessment.

We recently published a case study detailing how we partnered with an organization facing identical scalability constraints. They shifted their initial screening to a structured, automated format, allowing recruiters to skip repetitive early-stage interviews and focus entirely on high-value human evaluation.

The result was a dramatic lift in their hiring velocity and a much cleaner applicant conversion rate.

The full breakdown is attached. I’d love to get your take on their approach specifically how they structured the initial touchpoints.

Let me know if you’d be open to a quick chat later this week to sync on this.


Follow Up 3: Exit Email

Hi [First Name],

I reached out twice with relevant info and haven't heard back, so I’m assuming your priorities have shifted or the timing isn't right for [Prospect Company] to optimize your recruitment velocity at the moment.

I was really looking forward to helping your team address the scalability challenges we discussed, especially around targeting that 45% reduction in time-to-hire.

If this ever becomes a high priority for your talent acquisition strategy again, you know where to find me. In the meantime, I wish you and the team the absolute best.
